How to Start a Background Check Business Without Building the Back Office

October 31, 2026

If you are looking into how to start a background check business, you probably already see the demand. Employers hire, staffing companies place workers, and many of them screen candidates along the way. What stops people is everything behind the sale: the software, the order processing, the client support, the compliance work and the training.

This guide walks through what it actually takes to start a background screening company, which parts you need to own, and which parts you can hand to a partner so you can focus on selling.

Who this guide is for

It is written for three kinds of people:

  • Entrepreneurs who want a B2B business with repeat clients and are willing to do the selling.
  • Staffing company owners who already order screening for their own placements and want to offer it to clients under their own name.
  • Current or past background check salespeople who know the product and the buyer and want the client relationships to be theirs.

If you fit one of those, the hardest part (knowing how to talk to an employer about screening) may already be behind you.

What a background check business actually does

From the client's side, a screening company is simple. The client places an order, the candidate is screened, the report comes back and someone answers when the client has a question.

Behind that, there is a lot of moving machinery:

  • A software portal where clients order checks and see results
  • Processing of each check from order to finished report
  • Phone and email support for clients and their candidates
  • Compliance procedures, because companies that assemble reports for employment decisions are generally treated as consumer reporting agencies under the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)
  • Sales and account management to win and keep clients

When people say starting a screening company is hard, they usually mean that list. You can build every piece yourself, or you can partner with a company that already runs it.

Two ways to start: build it or plug into a back office

Building from scratch means choosing or developing software, setting up processing, staffing a support line, writing compliance procedures and learning all of it before your first client. It gives you full control, but it is slow and it pulls you away from selling.

Partnering with a turnkey back office means you own the company and the client relationships while the partner runs the operations behind your brand. That is the model Brango uses. Brango provides the software, the business setup and the training, answers all of your clients' calls and emails. You put your brand in front of the client and do what you are good at: building relationships and closing business.

Neither path is right for everyone. If you want to run operations, build. If you want to sell and grow a client base, a turnkey partner removes most of the setup work.

Step 1: Decide who you will sell to

Before anything else, pick a starting market. Good first markets are usually ones where you already have credibility:

  • Industries you have sold into before
  • Staffing companies and the employers they serve
  • Local businesses in your network that hire regularly

A narrow starting point makes your outreach sharper and your demos more relevant.

Step 2: Understand the compliance basics

Screening for employment is regulated. At a minimum, learn the FCRA basics: employers must give candidates a standalone written disclosure and get written permission before ordering a report, and there are notice steps before and after any adverse decision. State and local rules add more. The FTC and CFPB publish plain-language guidance, and it is worth reading before you talk to your first client.

You do not need to become a lawyer, but you should be able to explain the process confidently. Talk to a qualified attorney about your own situation, especially around licensing in your state.

Step 3: Set up your brand and portal

Clients buy from a company, not a person with a spreadsheet. You need a name, a simple website and a client-facing ordering portal with your brand on it. With a turnkey partner, the portal and the business setup are part of the package. Brango affiliates are typically up and running within 10 days of signing up.

Step 4: Learn to run a strong demo

Most screening sales come down to a demo: showing the client how ordering works, what the report looks like and what happens when they need help. Brango demos are held on Zoom, and in the early days you do not have to do them alone. Brango sits in on an affiliate's first 10 demos.

If you plan to hire salespeople, training does not stop after launch. Brango provides ongoing training for an affiliate's salespeople, so new hires learn the product the same way you did.

Step 5: Build your pipeline and keep clients

Winning a client is only half the job. Keeping one depends on what happens after the sale: how fast questions get answered and how smooth ordering feels. That is a hard part for a solo founder to cover. When Brango answers your clients' calls and emails, your time goes back into prospecting and relationships instead of support tickets.

Set a simple weekly rhythm: a block for outreach, a block for demos and a block for checking in with existing clients.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Spending months on setup before selling. The business is the client list, not the software.
  • Skipping compliance homework. Clients will ask, and you need clear answers.
  • Taking a former employer's accounts or confidential information. If you sold screening for someone else, read your agreements and get legal advice. Build your own client base.
  • Trying to be everything at once. Start with one market and grow from there.
