Referrals Still Win Screening Deals. Why That Favors Sales Reps Who Want to Own

November 17, 2026

If you sell background checks, you already know the best deals rarely start with a cold lead. They start with someone who trusts you telling someone else to call you. New industry research says that is still how this business works, and it may matter more in 2027 than it did before.

On November 12, 2026, IQubed Advisors published five findings from its 2026 CRA Marketplace Benchmark Survey, sponsored by Equifax. IQubed describes it as a blind, anonymous survey of 182 US-based professionals at background screening companies of every size. Here is what stood out, and what it means if you are a screening sales rep wondering whether to build something of your own.

What the survey found

A few findings are worth a close look:

  • Screening companies feel better about themselves than about the market. 87% rated their own business as strong or somewhat strong. Only 58% said the same about the broader market.
  • Pricing pressure is the profit constraint named most often. 56% put it in their top three.
  • Automation is about protecting margins, not filling seats. Operational automation was the top technology priority, even though 70% said talent is easy to attract and keep.
  • Companies are betting on identity products, but buyers have not caught up. 64% expect to sell significantly more identity and biometrics products in the next 12 months. Only 5% think buyers rank identity in their top three reasons for choosing a screening provider. One in three admitted they do not do a good job telling existing clients about new products.
  • Partner referrals bring the best leads by a wide margin. 50% named partner referrals as their highest quality lead source. No other channel reached 13%.

IQubed is an advisory firm that works with screening companies, and its own conclusion is that the market is entering what it calls an "Era of Displacement," where growth comes from winning business from competitors rather than waiting for hiring volume to come back.

Why this is good news for experienced sales reps

Read those findings together and a pattern shows up. The market is not going to hand anyone growth. Price is the easiest lever for a competitor to pull. Buyers do not yet understand some of the newer products. And trust, not ads, is what produces the best leads.

Every one of those points comes back to people, not software:

  1. Winning share means winning relationships. Clients switch screening providers when someone they trust gives them a reason to.
  2. Price matters less when service is strong. As IQubed puts it, the best defense against price cuts is a relationship and capabilities that make price a smaller part of the decision.
  3. New products need someone to explain them. If buyers do not see why identity checks matter, a good salesperson who can explain it plainly is worth more than another feature.
  4. Referrals come from reputation. The partners and clients who refer you are the ones who already know how you work.

If you have spent years selling screening, you have likely built exactly this: a book of relationships, a reputation for picking up the phone, and the ability to explain what a check does and does not cover.

The question is who owns those relationships

Here is the uncomfortable part. When you sell for someone else, the referrals you earn and the trust you build grow their company. That is fine for a while. But if the industry's best growth channel is relationships, the people who own the relationships are in a strong position to own the business too.

What usually stops a rep from making that move is not the selling. It is everything behind it: the platform, the business setup, answering client calls and email, and processing every order correctly. That is a full back office, and building one from scratch is a big lift for one person.
